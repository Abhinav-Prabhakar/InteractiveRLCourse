Distributional and
Risk-Sensitive RL

A value is not a number — it is a random variable. Track its whole distribution and you get to choose how much the bad tail matters.

01Return distributions

Every method so far chased a single number: the expected return. Distributional RL keeps the random variable itself,

$$Z^\pi(s,a)\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}R_{t+k+1}\quad\text{given } S_t=s,\;A_t=a,\qquad Q^\pi(s,a)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[Z^\pi(s,a)\right].$$

The distribution obeys a Bellman equation of its own, with equality in distribution:

$$Z^\pi(s,a)\;\overset{D}{=}\;R(s,a)+\gamma\,Z^\pi(S',A'),\qquad S'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,a),\; A'\sim\pi(\cdot\mid S').$$

Equal in distribution, not equal as numbers. Both sides are random variables, so the object of study is the whole law of $Z$, and the operator acts on entire distributions:

$$\left(\mathcal{T}^{\pi}Z\right)(s,a)\;\overset{D}{=}\;R(s,a)+\gamma\,Z(S',A').$$
The classical operator falls straight out. Taking expectations of both sides, $\mathbb{E}[(\mathcal{T}^\pi Z)(s,a)]=R(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'|s,a)\sum_{a'}\pi(a'|s')\mathbb{E}[Z(s',a')]$. The mean of the fixed point of $\mathcal{T}^\pi$ is exactly $Q^\pi$, so distributional RL strictly generalizes value-based RL.

Contraction survives, in the right metric. Let $d_p$ be the $p$-Wasserstein distance between distributions and $\bar d_p$ its sup over state–action pairs:

$$\bar d_p\!\left(\mathcal{T}^{\pi}Z_1,\;\mathcal{T}^{\pi}Z_2\right)\;\le\;\gamma\,\bar d_p\!\left(Z_1,\,Z_2\right),\qquad p\in[1,\infty).$$
The sup-norm that made value iteration a contraction is undefined between distributions. The Wasserstein metric takes its place, and the $\gamma$-contraction — hence a unique fixed point — survives.

02Representing the distribution

A distribution on a continuum of returns must be pinned down by finitely many parameters, and there are three standard answers.

C51 — categorical

Fix $K$ atoms on a bounded range and let the network emit a probability on each:

$$z_i=V_{\min}+(i-1)\Delta,\quad \Delta=\frac{V_{\max}-V_{\min}}{K-1},\qquad Z(s,a)=z_i\ \text{ with prob. } p_i(s,a).$$

The Bellman target lives off the grid, so it is projected back onto the atoms by splitting the mass of $r+\gamma z_j$ between its two nearest neighbours, giving the target distribution $m$ trained by cross-entropy:

$$\left(\Phi\,\hat{\mathcal{T}}Z\right)_i=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\Big[1-\tfrac{|r+\gamma z_j-z_i|}{\Delta}\Big]_0^1 p_j(s',a^*),\qquad L(\theta)=-\sum_{i=1}^{K}m_i\log p_i(s,a).$$

QR-DQN — quantile

Quantiles need no bounded support. Fix $N$ levels $\tau_i=(i-\tfrac12)/N$, learn their values $\theta_i(s,a)$, and fit with the pinball (quantile) loss on the Bellman target:

$$\rho^{1}_{\tau}(\delta)=\delta\left(\tau-\mathbf{1}\{\delta\lt0\}\right),\qquad L(\theta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\rho^{1}_{\tau_i}\!\left(r+\gamma\,z_{\tau_i}(s',a^*)-\theta_i(s,a)\right)\right].$$

IQN — implicit

Instead of fixing the levels, sample $\tau\sim U(0,1)$ and condition the network on it. One network then represents every quantile — a continuous distribution:

$$Z_\tau(s,a)=f\!\left(\psi(s,a)\odot\phi(\tau)\right),\qquad \phi(\tau)=\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(\sum_{i}\cos(\pi i\tau)\,w_i+b\right).$$

03Risk measures

A distribution is strictly richer information than its mean, but a controller needs one scalar to maximize. A risk measure $\rho$ collapses $Z$ to a number, and different $\rho$ give different policies from the very same $Z$.

The neutral choice is the mean; the simplest risk-aware one adds a penalty on spread:

$$\rho(Z)=\mathbb{E}[Z],\qquad \rho_\lambda(Z)=\mathbb{E}[Z]-\lambda\,\mathrm{Var}[Z],\qquad \lambda\ge 0.$$

CVaR (conditional value-at-risk) penalizes only the tail. At level $\alpha\in(0,1]$ it is the mean of the worst $\alpha$-fraction of outcomes:

$$\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(Z)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_0^{\alpha}F_Z^{-1}(u)\,du=\mathbb{E}\!\left[Z \mid Z\le \mathrm{VaR}_\alpha(Z)\right].$$

As $\alpha\to0$ it approaches the worst case; at $\alpha=1$ it is the mean.

Distorted expectations

The quantile function $F_Z^{-1}$ is the distribution. A distortion $h:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ that is nondecreasing with $h(0)=0,\;h(1)=1$ reweights the quantiles:

$$\rho_h(Z)=\int_0^1 F_Z^{-1}(u)\,dh(u).$$
This single formula contains the others. $h(u)=u$ gives the mean; $h(u)=\min(u/\alpha,\,1)$ gives exactly $\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha$; the Gaussian tilt $h(u)=\Phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(u)+\lambda\right)$ is the Wang transform. Because $h$ is a probability weighting, $\rho_h$ is a Choquet integral.

The explorer below uses the one-parameter family $h(u)=u^{\tau}$. It sweeps every attitude: $\tau=1$ is the mean, $\tau\lt 1$ steepens the weight near $u=0$ so the worst outcomes dominate (risk-averse), and $\tau\gt1$ pushes weight to $u=1$ onto the best outcomes (risk-seeking).

05Risk-sensitive policy gradients

The policy gradient maximizes $J(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_\pi[G]$. Swap the scalar return $G$ for a risk measure of $Z_\theta$ and the theorem is unchanged except in the weight it attaches to each return.

Mean–variance replaces $G$ by a penalized return. Since $\nabla_\theta\mathbb{E}[G^2]=\mathbb{E}[\nabla_\theta\log\pi\cdot G^2]$ and $\mathbb{E}[\nabla_\theta\log\pi]=0$, the variance gradient collapses to a squared-deviation weight:

$$\nabla_\theta\big(J-\lambda\,\mathrm{Var}[G]\big)=\mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(A\mid S)\left(G-\lambda\,(G-J)^2\right)\right].$$

CVaR keeps the likelihood-ratio estimator but credits only the worst $\alpha$-fraction of trajectories, with the threshold read off the learned distribution:

$$\nabla_\theta\,\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(Z_\theta)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(A\mid S)\;G\;\mathbf{1}\!\left\{G\le \mathrm{VaR}_\alpha(Z_\theta)\right\}\right].$$
You cannot reweight a tail you cannot see. Both estimators need $\mathrm{VaR}_\alpha$ or $\mathrm{Var}[G]$, which are properties of $Z$ — so the distribution is learned first and the risk-sensitive gradient is read off it.

The general distorted objective differentiates through the quantile function; sampling $\tau\sim U(0,1)$ turns the integral into a one-sample estimator weighted by $h'(\tau)$, exactly the IQN-style risk-sensitive policy gradient:

$$\rho_h(Z_\theta)=\int_0^1 F_\theta^{-1}(u)\,dh(u)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \nabla_\theta\,\rho_h=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim U(0,1)}\!\left[h'(\tau)\,\nabla_\theta F_\theta^{-1}(\tau)\right].$$
