Policy Gradient Methods

Differentiate the return with respect to the policy's own parameters and let gradient ascent find the policy — no max over actions required.

01The objective

Value-based methods learn $Q$ and read an action off by maximising. Policy-based methods parameterise the policy itself and ascend the return directly:

$$\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\ \text{(differentiable in }\theta),\qquad J(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim p_\theta}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^{t}\,r_{t+1}\right].$$

An episode is a trajectory $\tau=(s_0,a_0,s_1,a_1,\dots)$ drawn from

$$p_\theta(\tau)=\rho(s_0)\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\,P(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t).$$

Changing $\theta$ changes the actions and, through them, the states visited. The gradient of an expectation is not the expectation of the gradient here, because the measure $p_\theta$ moves with $\theta$.

02The policy gradient theorem

The log-derivative identity $\nabla_\theta p_\theta = p_\theta\,\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta$ moves the gradient inside the expectation:

$$\nabla_\theta J(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim p_\theta}\!\big[\,R(\tau)\,\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(\tau)\,\big].$$

Only the policy factors of $p_\theta(\tau)$ depend on $\theta$; the initial-state density and the transition model drop out:

$$\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(\tau)=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t).$$

The policy-only sum on that second line is exactly where the model is spent — no $\nabla_\theta P$ survives. Read it back: the gradient scores each action by how much $\theta$ raises its log-probability, weighted by the return the trajectory earned. Rewards before $a_t$ cannot depend on it, so they drop too, and averaging over the on-policy state distribution $\mu$ gives the theorem:

$$\nabla_\theta J(\theta)=\sum_{s}\mu(s)\sum_{a}\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\,q^\pi(s,a) =\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}\!\big[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)\,\hat q(S_t,A_t)\big].$$

The state distribution is not differentiated. That is the theorem's gift: $\mu$ shifts with $\theta$ in reality, but its derivative never appears, so the expectation can be sampled by simply acting with $\pi_\theta$.

03REINFORCE

Estimate the expectation with one sampled episode, and replace the action value by the return that followed it:

$$\nabla_\theta J(\theta)\approx\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\,G_t, \qquad \theta\leftarrow\theta+\alpha\,\widehat{\nabla_\theta J(\theta)}.$$

This is Monte-Carlo policy gradient — unbiased, but noisy. Each score is multiplied by a full sampled return whose spread grows with the horizon; a lucky episode drags every action it contains, good or bad, in its direction.

04Baselines and variance

Subtract any function $b(s)$ of the state. Its contribution to the gradient is exactly zero, because scores are mean-zero under $\pi_\theta$:

$$\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_\theta}\!\big[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\,b(s)\big] =b(s)\,\nabla_\theta\!\sum_{a}\pi_\theta(a\mid s)=b(s)\,\nabla_\theta 1=0.$$

So replacing $G_t$ by $G_t-b(s_t)$ leaves the mean untouched and only shrinks the spread. The natural baseline is the value $V^\pi(s)$ — the mean of $G_t$, the part of the return the score cannot explain. Any $b$ is unbiased; a good one is what turns REINFORCE from a curiosity into an algorithm.

05Actor–critic

Rather than sample the whole return, learn its mean. An actor $\pi_\theta$ chooses actions; a critic $V_w$ estimates value; and the policy is nudged by the advantage, which the one-step TD error estimates:

$$A^\pi(s,a)=Q^\pi(s,a)-V^\pi(s),\qquad \delta_t=r_{t+1}+\gamma V_w(s_{t+1})-V_w(s_t)\approx A^\pi(s_t,a_t).$$
$$\theta\leftarrow\theta+\alpha_\theta\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\,\delta_t, \qquad w\leftarrow w+\alpha_w\,\delta_t\,\nabla_w V_w(s_t).$$

The advantage is centered under the policy: $\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[A^\pi(s,a)]=0$. Subtracting $V^\pi$ removes exactly the part of the return the score cannot influence, so the actor moves only on what it controls. The critic trades Monte-Carlo bias for bootstrapping variance — it reacts at every step, but only as far as its own errors allow.

06Advantage estimation: GAE(λ)

Set $\delta_t=r_{t+1}+\gamma V(s_{t+1})-V(s_t)$. The advantage that trades off horizon is a geometric sum of TD errors:

$$\hat A^{\mathrm{GAE}(\lambda)}_t=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\lambda)^{l}\,\delta_{t+l} =\delta_t+\gamma\lambda\,\hat A^{\mathrm{GAE}(\lambda)}_{t+1}.$$

At $\lambda=0$ it is the one-step TD advantage — low variance, biased by whatever the critic gets wrong. At $\lambda=1$ it is the Monte-Carlo advantage — unbiased, high variance. $\lambda$ slides continuously between the two.

07Policy Gradient Playground

A $5\times5$ grid, start bottom-left, goal top-right, a pit at the centre. Every state carries its own softmax policy over the four moves, and REINFORCE updates only its logits, from sampled episodes alone:

$$\theta_{s,a}\leftarrow\theta_{s,a}+\alpha\,(G_t-b)\, \big(\mathbf 1\{a=A_t\}-\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\big),$$

where the bracket is $\nabla_{\theta_{s,a}}\log\pi_\theta(A_t\mid s)$ and $b$ is the running mean return. Train it and watch each cell's arrows rotate toward the goal and away from the pit — the policy distribution is the thing being learned.

Cheat sheet

$$\begin{aligned} \nabla_\theta J(\theta) &= \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}\!\big[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)\,\hat q(S_t,A_t)\big]\\[2pt] \text{REINFORCE}\quad \theta &\leftarrow \theta+\alpha\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\,G_t\\[2pt] \text{baseline}\quad &\mathbb{E}\!\big[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\,b(s)\big]=0\\[2pt] A^\pi(s,a) &= Q^\pi(s,a)-V^\pi(s)\ \approx\ \delta_t=r_{t+1}+\gamma V(s_{t+1})-V(s_t)\\[2pt] \hat A^{\mathrm{GAE}(\lambda)}_t &= \delta_t+\gamma\lambda\,\hat A^{\mathrm{GAE}(\lambda)}_{t+1} \end{aligned}$$
