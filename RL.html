Markov Reward Processes
& Markov Decision Processes

The mathematical objects underneath every RL algorithm — built up one piece at a time, with every transition probability you can poke at.

01The Markov property

A stochastic process has the Markov property when the future is conditionally independent of the past given the present:

$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(S_{t+1}=s' \;\middle|\; S_t=s,\; S_{t-1}=s_{t-1},\dots,S_0=s_0\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(S_{t+1}=s' \;\middle|\; S_t=s\right)$$

Equivalently: the state is a sufficient statistic for the future. Knowing where you are makes how you got there irrelevant.

Why this single assumption carries the whole theory. It lets us write the expected return from a state as a function of that state alone, $V(s)$. Without it, value would have to depend on the entire history, and nothing that follows would close.

02Markov chains

A Markov chain is the bare skeleton: a set of states $\mathcal{S}$ and a transition matrix $P$ whose entries are

$$P_{ss'}=\mathbb{P}\!\left(S_{t+1}=s' \mid S_t=s\right),\qquad P_{ss'}\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{s'\in\mathcal{S}}P_{ss'}=1 .$$

Every row of $P$ is a probability distribution. The $n$-step transition matrix is just $P^n$, and a distribution $\pi$ is stationary if $\pi^\top P=\pi^\top$.

03Markov Reward Processes

Attach a reward to being in each state and you get a Markov Reward Process $\;(\mathcal{S},P,R,\gamma)$.

$$R_s=\mathbb{E}\!\left[R_{t+1}\mid S_t=s\right]$$

The return and the discount

The return $G_t$ is the total discounted reward from time $t$ onward:

$$G_t \;=\; R_{t+1}+\gamma R_{t+2}+\gamma^2R_{t+3}+\cdots \;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}R_{t+k+1}$$

The discount $\gamma\in[0,1]$ does two jobs: below $1$ it keeps the sum finite, and it encodes how much we care about the future. Drag the slider: at $\gamma=0$ only the first reward survives.

The value function

The state-value function is the expected return starting from $s$:

$$V(s)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[G_t \mid S_t=s\right]$$

Deriving the Bellman expectation equation

Split the return into the immediate reward plus the discounted rest, then use the Markov property to throw away the history:

$$ \begin{aligned} V(s) &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[G_t \mid S_t=s\right] \\[2pt] &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[R_{t+1}+\gamma G_{t+1} \mid S_t=s\right] \\[2pt] &= R_s+\gamma\sum_{s'\in\mathcal{S}}P_{ss'}\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[G_{t+1}\mid S_{t+1}=s'\right] \\[2pt] &= R_s+\gamma\sum_{s'\in\mathcal{S}}P_{ss'}\,V(s') \end{aligned} $$

The third line is exactly where the Markov property is spent: conditioning on the whole history collapses to conditioning on $S_{t+1}=s'$.

Solving it in closed form

Written over the whole state space, the Bellman equation is a linear system:

$$V=R+\gamma PV \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (I-\gamma P)V=R \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\;V=(I-\gamma P)^{-1}R\;}$$

Because $P$ is stochastic, $\|\gamma P\|_\infty=\gamma<1$, so the Neumann series converges and the inverse always exists:

$$(I-\gamma P)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}P^{k} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad V=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}P^{k}R$$

Read that last line back: $V$ is the sum over horizons $k$ of the $k$-step reward, discounted by $\gamma^k$ and averaged over $P^k$.

04Markov Decision Processes

Now give the agent a choice. An MDP is $\;(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,R,\gamma)$ where the transition and reward now depend on the action taken:

$$P^{a}_{ss'}=\mathbb{P}\!\left(S_{t+1}=s'\mid S_t=s,\,A_t=a\right),\qquad R^{a}_{s}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[R_{t+1}\mid S_t=s,\,A_t=a\right]$$

An MRP is precisely an MDP where every state has exactly one available action. That is the entire difference, and the hard part: choosing means maximising, and $\max$ is not linear.

Policies

A policy $\pi(a\mid s)$ is a distribution over actions for each state; it turns the MDP back into an MRP. Averaging over actions gives the policy-induced transition and reward:

$$P^{\pi}_{ss'}=\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid s)\,P^{a}_{ss'},\qquad R^{\pi}_{s}=\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid s)\,R^{a}_{s}$$

Two value functions

$$V_{\pi}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[G_t\mid S_t=s\right],\qquad Q_{\pi}(s,a)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[G_t\mid S_t=s,\,A_t=a\right]$$

They are related by a simple average and a simple lookahead:

$$V_{\pi}(s)=\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid s)\,Q_{\pi}(s,a),\qquad Q_{\pi}(s,a)=R^{a}_{s}+\gamma\sum_{s'}P^{a}_{ss'}\,V_{\pi}(s')$$

Bellman expectation equation (policy evaluation)

$$V_{\pi}(s)=\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid s)\left[R^{a}_{s}+\gamma\sum_{s'}P^{a}_{ss'}\,V_{\pi}(s')\right]$$

For a fixed policy this is again linear — $V_\pi=(I-\gamma P^{\pi})^{-1}R^{\pi}$ — so evaluating a policy is an MRP solve.

Optimality

Define $V_{*}(s)=\max_{\pi}V_{\pi}(s)$ and $Q_{*}(s,a)=\max_{\pi}Q_{\pi}(s,a)$. They satisfy the Bellman optimality equations:

$$V_{*}(s)=\max_{a}\left[R^{a}_{s}+\gamma\sum_{s'}P^{a}_{ss'}\,V_{*}(s')\right]$$ $$Q_{*}(s,a)=R^{a}_{s}+\gamma\sum_{s'}P^{a}_{ss'}\max_{a'}Q_{*}(s',a')$$

and the optimal policy is greedy with respect to $Q_*$:

$$\pi_{*}(s)=\arg\max_{a}\;Q_{*}(s,a)$$
The $\max$ is the whole story. In an MRP the Bellman equation is linear and has a closed-form solution. In an MDP the $\max$ makes it piecewise-linear and nonlinear — no matrix inverse solves it. Instead we iterate the operator $\mathcal{T}$ defined by the right-hand side, which is a $\gamma$-contraction: $$\|\mathcal{T}V-\mathcal{T}U\|_{\infty}\le\gamma\|V-U\|_{\infty}.$$ Banach's fixed-point theorem then guarantees a unique fixed point and geometric convergence at rate $\gamma$ — that's value iteration.

05A slippery gridworld

A $5\times5$ grid. The agent moves up, right, down, left, but the world is slippery: the intended action succeeds with probability $0.8$, and with probability $0.1$ each the agent slides perpendicular instead. Bumping a wall leaves you in place.

$$\mathbb{P}(s'\mid s,a)=\begin{cases}0.8 & s'=\text{intended}(s,a)\\ 0.1 & s'=\text{perpendicular}(s,a)\ \text{(each)}\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$

Reward: $-0.04$ per step, $+1$ for reaching the goal (top-right), $-1$ for the pit (centre). Both terminal. The slip makes any route brushing the pit a gamble.

Cheat sheet

$$\begin{aligned} G_t &= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}R_{t+k+1}\\[2pt] V_{\pi}(s) &= \sum_{a}\pi(a\mid s)\left[R^{a}_{s}+\gamma\sum_{s'}P^{a}_{ss'}V_{\pi}(s')\right]\\[2pt] V_{*}(s) &= \max_{a}\left[R^{a}_{s}+\gamma\sum_{s'}P^{a}_{ss'}V_{*}(s')\right]\\[2pt] \pi_{*}(s) &= \arg\max_{a}\left[R^{a}_{s}+\gamma\sum_{s'}P^{a}_{ss'}V_{*}(s')\right] \end{aligned}$$

Everything downstream — TD learning, Q-learning, policy gradients, PPO — is an approximate way of computing, sampling, or differentiating one of these four lines.
